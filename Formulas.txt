Because both lists advance by one step every time, and both stems and branches must always share the same Yin/Yang polarity, the cycle produces exactly 60 unique combinations before resetting. Common Calculation Mistakes
Counting to the Wrong Solar Term
Many beginners use the mid-term markers (中气) instead of the major nodes (节). The Da Yun formula uses only the 节 markers, not the 中气. Using the wrong boundary will throw off your starting age by several months or more.

Confusing Forward and Reverse
The most frequent error is applying the wrong direction. A female born in a Yang year uses forward direction — she counts to the next solar term, not the previous one. Double-check your birth year's Heavenly Stem polarity before determining direction.

Using Lunar Calendar Dates
The solar term boundaries are based on the solar calendar (公历), not the lunar calendar. Always use the Gregorian calendar date to count days to the solar term node. The solar term dates themselves are fixed in the solar calendar within a one or two day variation each year.

Rounding Instead of Using Remainder
Some practitioners round 19 days to "6 years" and ignore the fractional months. The correct approach preserves the remainder: 19 days = 6 years and 4 months. This precision matters when interpreting transitions between Luck Pillars.

Reverse Direction: Counting Backward
When the direction is reverse (逆行), you count backward from your birth date to the previous major solar term. The formula is the same: days ÷ 3, with the remainder converting to months.

In San Yuan practice, tables are mapped against sixty-year Jiazi cycles, divided into Upper, Middle, and Lower Eras (Yuan):

* Align the precise computed Gregorian date and minute of the Jie (specifically Lichun or Dongzhi) with the corresponding stem-branch (Ganzhi) of the year and day.
* Because the Earth's orbit is elliptical, the time interval between successive solar terms is variable (ranging roughly from 14 to 16 days), meaning precise hour and minute validation near midnight is critical to prevent shifting a day pillar incorrectly. [[1](https://ytliu0.github.io/ChineseCalendar/sunMoon.html), [2](https://ytliu0.github.io/ChineseCalendar/solarTerms.html)]

The Sanyuan Futou Table (三元符头表) is a vital lookup tool in Qimen Dunjia (奇门遁甲). It aligns the 60 Sexagenary Cycle (Jiazi) pairs into three 5-day cycles called Upper Yuan (上元), Middle Yuan (中元), and Lower Yuan (下元).
Each 5-day cycle is led by a specific heavenly stem known as the Futou (符头 - Leader / Stem-Head). The Futou is always a Jia (甲) or a Ji (己) stem.

## Key Rules for Reading the Table

* Spotting the Futou: Look at the Day Stem of the day you are analyzing. Trace backwards to find the nearest previous day that starts with a Jia (甲) or Ji (己). That day is your Futou.
* The Sanyuan Cycle:
* Days starting with Jia (甲) or Ji (己) paired with Zi (子), Wu (午), Mao (卯), You (酉) always begin the Upper Yuan.
* Days starting with Jia (甲) or Ji (己) paired with Yin (寅), Shen (申), Si (巳), Hai (亥) always begin the Middle Yuan.
* Days starting with Jia (甲) or Ji (己) paired with Chen (辰), Xu (戌), Chou (丑), Wei (未) always begin the Lower Yuan.
